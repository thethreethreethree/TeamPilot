# Think1 — How We Build Here (Part 1: The Rules)

> Moses — read this first. It's short on purpose. These are the rules
> everyone here builds by. They're written plainly so you can start
> using them today. You don't need to memorize them; the Coach will
> remind you. Just know they exist and that they matter more than
> writing code fast.

These rules exist because the fastest way to waste a week is to build
the wrong thing confidently. A wrong idea built quickly is still wrong —
you just arrive at "wrong" sooner. So we slow down at the start to be
sure we understand, and that saves far more time than it costs.

---

## Rule 1 — Understand before you build
Before you write any code, be able to say *why* the problem exists and
*what* the change is for. If you can't explain it in your own words,
you don't understand it yet — and you're not ready to build. Ask the
Coach. Ask questions. Understanding first is not slower; it's how you
avoid building the wrong thing.

## Rule 2 — Ask before you assume
If something is unclear, **ask** — don't guess and quietly do it your
own way. A guess that's wrong costs more than a question. There are no
dumb questions here; there are only expensive silent assumptions.

## Rule 3 — Always explain the WHY
When you make a change, say *why* you made it, not just *what* you did.
The reason is the part that teaches — to you later, and to the next
person. A change with no stated reason is unfinished work.

## Rule 4 — Don't break other things
Before you change something, look at what else uses it. A change that
fixes one screen but breaks another is not a fix. When you touch
shared code, ask: "what else depends on this?" — and check it.

## Rule 5 — Test it before you say it works
"It should work" is not "it works." Run it. Look at it with your own
eyes. Only call something done after you've seen it do the right thing.
If you haven't tested it, say so honestly — say "untested."

## Rule 6 — Save your work the right way
When a piece of work is done and tested, save it (commit it) with a
short note explaining what and why. Small, clear saves beat one giant
messy one. The Coach will show you exactly how.

## Rule 7 — If a fix fails twice, STOP
If you try to fix something and it fails, then you try again and it
fails again — stop. Trying the same idea harder won't help. A repeated
failure means your *understanding* of the problem is wrong, not your
typing. Go back to Rule 1: re-understand the problem from scratch.

## Rule 8 — Be honest about what you don't know
Nobody expects you to know everything — you're learning. What's
expected is honesty: "I'm not sure this works," "I didn't test this
part," "I don't understand why this happens yet." Honesty is how you
earn trust here, and it's how the Coach can actually help you.

---

**The one sentence to remember:** *Understand it, ask when unsure,
explain your why, don't break neighbors, test before you claim, save
cleanly, and stop when you're looping.*

The Coach (type **"I'm Moses"** when you open the project) will walk you
through all of this, one step at a time. You are not alone in here.
