# CLOSURE — the advice I withdrew

## What is true now

Pattern Interrupt has been rendered in both its states and the demo brief no longer tells the
founder to avoid it. The caution that replaced the old one is narrower and accurate: the surface is
sound, its data is untested.

## What happened

I wrote a readiness brief for an investor presentation. In it I said Pattern Interrupt was "the
surface I would be least willing to demo live", resting on two facts that are true and verified —
detection has only ever run inside the scoring route, and no pattern has ever opened.

Neither fact is about the screen.

A surface with no data still has an empty state, and an empty state is a designed artefact. This
one reads:

> **Nothing has been scored yet**
> Patterns come from scored pitches. Once your recordings are scored against the rubric, repeated
> misses show up here.

Four counters at 0, each captioned with what it would mean. An explainer that teaches the feature
in one sentence. Nothing invented, nothing hidden.

That is §3.4 rendered — *"a system that behaved identically for every customer on install would be
claiming understanding it cannot have — a lie. Refuse to build that."* An investor asking "what
happens on day one?" is answered better by that screen than by a slide, and I advised skipping it.

## The shape of the mistake

A fact about DATA, promoted to a judgement about a SURFACE.

It is the session's own recurring finding turned on its author. All day the pattern has been:
someone reasons from something true to something confident and unobserved — a gate that checks a
nearby question, a test that measures nothing, a cast asserting a shape the route does not send. Mine
was the same move, made about a screenshot I had not taken.

The cost of taking it was about five minutes. The cost of the retraction was larger, and it landed
on a document the founder was going to act on.
