# REMEDIATE

### A judgement about a surface, derived from a fact about its data

gate-or-promise: promise

No gate. "Did you look at the thing you are advising about" is not a checkable property, and
building a tracker that pretends to check it would be decoration.

**The promise:** a claim about how a screen BEHAVES or how it would LAND requires rendering it.
A claim about its data does not — and the two must not be swapped. "No pattern has ever opened" is
a data fact and it was true. "I would not demo this" is a surface judgement and it was wrong, and
the distance between them is the whole error.

The practical form, since a capture now costs about five minutes: **before advising against showing
something, capture it.** That is cheaper than the retraction.

### The correction had to replace the claim, not sit after it

gate-or-promise: declined

No gate and no further action — recording the editorial rule because it nearly went the other way.

A section 7 correction appended while sections 3 and 5 still said "avoid this" would leave a reader who stops
early with exactly the advice being withdrawn. Both were amended to point forward. A document that
contradicts itself is worse than one that is simply wrong, because the wrong one can at least be
corrected in the reader's head.

### The fixture proves the surface, not the data

gate-or-promise: declined

Nothing to fix. It is a limit of what a capture can witness, stated at the point of the claim in
both check.md and the brief's section 7.

Worth keeping visible next to this build's conclusion: the correction narrows the caution from "do
not demo this" to "the surface is sound and its data is untested". Deleting the caution entirely
would have been the over-correction, and it would have been the same error in the other direction —
a confident claim about something unobserved.
