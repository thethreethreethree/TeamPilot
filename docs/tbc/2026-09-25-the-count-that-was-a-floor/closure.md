# CLOSURE — the count that was a floor

## What is true now

The rep's door-logging screen shows four tiles instead of one, a door glyph with its ring, and two
buttons instead of one-and-a-ghost. Its recording state has been photographed for the first time.

And the class finally has an honest number: **80 sites across 20 files reachable from a Sales Coach
route**, of which 6 are closed here and **74 remain**.

## What the number replaced

Every per-file count in every closure today — "nine in `/settings`", "six in `/training`" — was a
count of a FILE, reported as a count of a SCREEN. The sweeps grepped the route's own file and never
what it mounts; the fixes replaced one exact alpha string and passed silently over `[0.03]`.

`DoorLog.tsx` is what that cost. Eleven sites in the rep's core loop, in a component I rendered
this morning and recorded as clean.

## Two reasons I looked at it and saw nothing

**Four of the eleven were in the state I photographed.** The other seven were in `recording`,
`outcome` and `naming` — a state machine has four screens and the capture drove one.

**And the four I did see, I read as intentional.** Three KPI tiles had no card; the fourth,
accented one did. Three bare numbers beside one card looks like emphasis. **Four bare numbers would
have looked like a bug.** The presence of the correct one is what disguised the other three, and
only the dark capture — where all four have cards — settles it.

That is the argument for the pair being the method, made better by a case where the single image
was not merely uninformative but actively misleading.

## Three scanners, three wrong numbers

94 → 82 → 80, the first counting the `dark:` halves of my own mode-splits and the second counting
**matches inside code comments describing values my fixes had replaced** — this morning's
documentation, reported as defects by my own tool.

That is the third scanner today after the wrong-namespace probe (0 for a class of 56) and the
accent scan (18 for a class of 5). All three caught by reading the members. **A count is a claim
about the tool until someone has read what it found.**
